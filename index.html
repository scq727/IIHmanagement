Weight Management
Weight loss is a primary treatment — not adjunctive
A 10% reduction in body weight is associated with significant reduction in LP OP and papilledema grade. Weight loss is the only treatment with potential for disease remission without medication.
Counsel on 10% weight loss target at every visitEven modest loss (5–10 kg) can reduce papilledema within weeks
Refer to weight management clinicStructured program outperforms advice alone — place referral today if not enrolled
Consider GLP-1 agonist (semaglutide, liraglutide)Emerging evidence for ICP reduction beyond weight loss alone; check insurance coverage
Bariatric surgery referral for BMI ≥40 or ≥35 with comorbiditiesMost durable weight loss intervention — can produce IIH remission

RNFL — Surgical Escalation Threshold
Compare baseline to current OCT — flags >5 µm thinning
Baseline = first visit OCT (edematous). Thinning >5 µm regardless of absolute value = surgical escalation criterion met.
Surgical Escalation
⚠ Indications for surgical escalation
▾
Any one criterion warrants surgical referral discussion:
  • VA <20/200 despite ≥4 weeks maximal medical therapy
  • Progressive VF loss threatening fixation on serial HVF
  • RNFL thinning >5 µm from baseline on serial OCT
  • Fulminant IIH at presentation (Grade 4–5 disc edema, acute vision loss)
  • Intolerance to all medical therapies with persistent vision risk
  • Failure of serial therapeutic LPs to maintain ICP control
👁 ONSF — Optic Nerve Sheath Fenestration
▾
Prefer when: Vision loss is primary; headache secondary or absent · Unilateral or asymmetric loss · Pregnancy (vision-threatening) · Prior shunt failure
Mechanism: Decompresses optic nerve subarachnoid space locally. Does not reliably reduce systemic ICP — headache may persist or recur.
Post-op monitoring:
1 WEEK
VA, confrontation VF, disc exam, diplopia check
1 MONTH
VA, formal HVF 24-2, OCT RNFL vs. pre-op
3 MONTHS
Full reassessment; RNFL trend from pre-op baseline
ANNUAL
OCT RNFL trend; reassess ICP control; photo comparison
Failure criteria: No VA/VF improvement at 4–6 weeks, or recurrent vision loss after initial improvement → repeat ONSF vs. CSF diversion.
Complications: Diplopia (superior oblique), pupillary asymmetry, CSF leak, infection, worsening vision (rare, <1%).
🧠 CSF Shunting — LP or VP Shunt
▾
Prefer when: Headache is primary disabling symptom · Bilateral severe vision loss · Failed ONSF · Need ongoing ICP monitoring via programmable valve
LP vs. VP shunt: LP shunt is less invasive and preferred first-line at many centers; VP shunt may have lower long-term revision rates. Neurosurgery preference and body habitus guide choice.
Post-op monitoring:
2 WEEKS
Headache response, wound, new neuro symptoms
1 MONTH
VA, HVF, disc exam; LP OP if symptoms persist
3 MONTHS
Full ophthalmic reassessment; OCT RNFL trend
ANNUAL
Shunt function review; low threshold for imaging if symptoms recur
Failure modes:
  • Obstruction (most common) — recurrent headache, vision loss → shunt series CT/MRI
  • Over-drainage — positional headache worse upright → programmable valve adjustment
  • Infection — fever, meningismus → emergency neurosurgery referral
  • Migration — confirmed on imaging → neurosurgical revision
Revision rate: LP shunts ~50% at 5 years; VP shunts ~30–40%. Counsel patients.
🩸 TVS Stenting — Venous Sinus Stenting
▾
Consider when: Bilateral transverse sinus stenosis on MRV · High venous pressure gradient (≥8 mmHg on venography) · Failed / intolerant to medical therapy · Younger patient, prefer to avoid shunt
Refer to: Neuro-interventional radiology. Diagnostic cerebral venography required first to confirm gradient and guide stent sizing.
Antiplatelet therapy: Aspirin + clopidogrel ×3–6 months post-stent, then aspirin indefinitely. Confirm regimen with neuro-IR team.
Post-procedure monitoring:
1 MONTH
VA, HVF, disc exam; headache response; antiplatelet compliance
3 MONTHS
Full ophthalmic assessment; OCT RNFL vs. baseline
6 MONTHS
MRV for stent patency; LP OP if symptoms persist
ANNUAL
Long-term patency; antiplatelet review
Failure modes:
  • In-stent restenosis — recurrent symptoms; MRV confirms → repeat venography ± re-stenting
  • Incomplete ICP reduction — partial correction; consider additional therapy or shunt
  • Stent thrombosis (rare) — neurological deterioration → emergency neuro-IR

Frisén Scale — Papilledema Grading
● Grade 0 — Normal
▾
Key features: Sharp disc margins 360°. Normal NFL reflex temporal. Normal C/D ratio. No elevation.

OCT RNFL: Within normal limits. Use as your baseline for all future comparisons.

Clinical note: Document disc photos at diagnosis and every visit. Grade 0 on treatment = treatment success endpoint.
● Grade 1 — Minimal
▾
Key features: C-shaped halo of blurred NFL at nasal pole. Temporal margin sharp. No visible elevation on casual inspection. Easy to miss.

OCT RNFL: Mildly thickened nasally. May fall within "normal" range on some instruments — compare to prior scan.

Pitfall: Most commonly missed grade. If uncertain between Grade 0 and Grade 1, photograph and refer — never dismiss. Peripapillary OCT is more sensitive than clinical exam at this grade.
● Grade 2 — Low degree
▾
Key features: Circumferential halo of blurred NFL 360°. Nasal border clearly elevated. Temporal margin still relatively sharp. No major vessel obscuration at disc margin.

OCT RNFL: Clearly thickened (>120 µm average expected).

Clinical note: Most common grade at IIH presentation. Vision typically preserved (20/40 or better). Most patients at this grade respond to medical therapy. Begin acetazolamide and weight management.
● Grade 3 — Moderate
▾
Key features: Disc elevation with obscuration of one or more segments of major vessels at the disc margin. NFL halo 360°. Paton lines (concentric peripapillary retinal folds) may appear.

OCT RNFL: Markedly thickened with significant elevation artifact on peripapillary scan.

Clinical action: Formal HVF 24-2 urgently. MRI/MRV if not done. Start or escalate acetazolamide at this visit — do not defer. Follow up in 4–6 weeks, not 3 months.
● Grade 4 — Marked
▾
Key features: Complete obscuration of a major vessel segment on the disc surface. Prominent disc surface elevation. Flame hemorrhages. Paton lines.

OCT RNFL: Severely thickened; segmentation artifacts common — interpret with caution.

Clinical action: Same-day urgent referral or ER routing. Therapeutic LP at this visit if VA declining. Begin acetazolamide immediately unless contraindicated. Low threshold for ONSF/shunt referral.

Do not: Send this patient home without a concrete plan. Grade 4 with good VA today can progress to severe loss within days.
● Grade 5 — Severe / Fulminant
▾
Key features: Dome-shaped disc protrusion. Complete vessel obscuration. Extensive hemorrhages, exudates. Macular edema possible. Retinal folds extending into macula.

Clinical action: Emergency. Same-day therapeutic LP and emergent neurosurgery / neuro-ophthalmology consultation. Direct ER referral with your clinical note if specialist unavailable today. IV acetazolamide if oral not possible.

Note: Some centers use Frisén Grade 4 as the maximum. IIHTT used a modified 0–5 scale. Confirm grading convention with your referral center.
